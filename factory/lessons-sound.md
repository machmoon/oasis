- Each control must change the core of the sound it names, not just switch a secondary layer on or off. Check that its extremes measurably shift the main event's spectrum and envelope, and that it does not duplicate another knob.
- When adding a secondary layer such as liquid, debris or rattle, band-limit and shape its noise to the material. Also check that the layer stays subordinate to the main event, and that it disappears when the parameter that justifies it (here, fullness) goes to zero. Raw white noise layered on a physical impact almost always reads as hiss rather than as the material.
- When a brief names a specific sound source (e.g. 'on parked cars'), synthesise it as its own recognisable layer with a visible signature, such as tonal resonances or distinct impacts. Do not fold it into generic noise. Buyers pay for the named detail, and its absence makes the product generic.
- Trim the render buffer to the actual audible end (last sample above roughly −60 dB plus a short safety fade) instead of allocating a fixed or worst-case length. Large trailing silence is a defect buyers will notice immediately in every instance.
- When a knob offers discrete size choices, make each step change at least two structural features (duration, low-frequency weight, number or spacing of events), not just level or brightness, and check every option side by side so that no two read as the same sound.
- Every choice option and toggle must produce a clearly visible difference in envelope, density or spectrum. Before shipping, render each option side by side and remove or redesign any knob whose renders look interchangeable.
- When a render is assembled from segments, loops or crossfaded halves, inspect the waveform at every join and use equal-power, phase-aware crossfades: a level notch at a splice point is audible and repeats in every render that uses that structure.
- Make each continuous knob change the structure of the sound across its range, such as event density, added layers or spectral bands, not just its gain. Then render each knob's extremes on their own to confirm the change is clearly visible, rather than only checking that something renders.
- Before submitting, render each knob at its extremes and confirm the change is visible in the spectrogram or the numbers. Pitch effects like Doppler must show as moving lines, tonal sources must show as harmonics, and toggles must alter the envelope. A knob whose effect cannot be seen should be fixed or removed.
- When a size or intensity control is meant to make a sound 'bigger', scale the audible main body (duration, low-end mass, density) rather than appending sparse trailing events. Any toggle that removes a section must hand off with a short fade, never a hard cut.
- When a sound is defined by an electrical or mechanical source, build its core from that source's actual spectral signature (e.g. a harmonic buzz comb at mains frequency and its overtones), and add noise only as texture on top. Gated broadband noise reads as generic static at every setting.
- When a knob names a distinct layer (like rain over a hum), render that knob alone at its maximum and confirm its own signature is plainly visible over everything else. If you cannot point to it in the spectrogram, the layer is too quiet, masked, or not wired up.
- When a brief names a spatial or acoustic effect (echo, room, distance), make it visibly distinct in the render: discrete or clearly smeared reflections, not just a longer fade. Also check every render for broadband vertical lines that signal clicks from unsmoothed parameter or envelope jumps.
- Make sure the event named in the title is clearly visible and audible at defaults, and never let a single isolated transient set the peak normalisation. When the background layers are quiet, cap or smooth stray impulses so they do not become a click that dominates the whole file.
- Every choice option must produce a render that is clearly different in envelope or spectrum, not just a few hundred Hz of centroid. If you can't name the visual difference between two options, change the underlying acoustics (space, distance, reflections) until you can.
- Cap density, overlap and bed level so that the sound's defining transients stay clearly above any background layer at every combination of knob extremes, and never peak-normalise a sound whose brief calls for it to be quiet.
- Make the defining event in the brief (here the zap or crackle) the most visible feature in the spectrogram at defaults and at every extreme, and derive every stage, including the tail, from the same source rather than appending a generic decaying tone.
- Any continuous or ambient sound (hums, beds, weather, room tone) must expose a duration knob with a default of at least 10 s, and its no-tail mode must be built to loop seamlessly by matching start and end state. A fixed short render with a fade is not a usable bed.
- When a sound is built from joined segments or a toggled envelope stage, render it and inspect the joins for notches, dropouts or discontinuities. Crossfade every boundary with equal-power overlap so that no edit point is visible in the waveform.
- Liquid sounds are not shaped noise: build water from many short, pitched, randomly timed droplet/bubble events layered over a mass component, and make sure every control knob (like an impact or slap) produces a clearly visible transient in the render at its maximum.
- Every toggle and knob must produce a visible, purpose-specific change in the render: a loop toggle must change the ending (a seamless wrap vs a natural fade-out), and each layer knob should add its own identifiable band or texture rather than just shifting overall brightness or level.
- Whenever a synth switches segments, pitches, voices or envelope stages mid-sound, crossfade or ramp over a few milliseconds and zero-cross the edges. Then inspect every render's spectrogram for thin full-height vertical lines, which are clicks, before submitting.
- Derive render length from the parameters that control event timing, such as speed, distance and size, so that at every extreme the event completes and decays to silence inside the file. Compensate level across the range so that extreme settings don't jump far above or below the default loudness.
- For liquid sounds, broadband noise alone reads as wind or a whoosh. Add discrete resonant events (short pitched bubble chirps and gurgles that sweep in frequency) and check that they show up as tonal streaks in the spectrogram before submitting.
- Never switch a sound on or off with an instant gate: put a few-millisecond fade on every burst edge, and make sure the last event fully decays before the render ends, so stutters don't click and files don't end abruptly.
- Every option of a choice knob must change the sound's defining structure (event duration, spacing, envelope, reflections), not just nudge the tone; render each option side by side and confirm a listener could name which is which, and render any toggle on and off to prove it audibly does something.
- Every element named in the title or brief must be clearly audible and visible at default settings. Defaults are the product demo, so a secondary layer such as rain or crowd should not be buried until the user pushes its knob.
- For any loopable ambience, compare the first and last ~200 ms of the render for matching level and event density before shipping. Keep a continuous low bed under sparse events so that minimum settings never produce silent gaps.
- Model resonances as a response to the excitation, not as an independent oscillator. Drive pipe or body modes from the impacts and noise bursts so they flare and decay with each hit. Running them as constant sine partials makes them read as a synthetic drone.
- When a sound's identity rests on one physical cue (Doppler drop, pitch sweep, transient), make that cue unmistakable at default settings and check that it shows up visibly in the spectrogram. A matching envelope alone does not make the sound read correctly.
- For sounds built from sparse events over a background bed, keep the bed well below the events at every knob extreme, and give each event its defining feature (here a short pitched transient) with randomized timing. Then confirm that every toggle and knob produces a visibly different render; otherwise remove it.
- Render every knob at its minimum and confirm it is still a quieter or smaller version of the same sound. Low ends of density or flow parameters must thin the texture gradually, not collapse it into isolated clicks and gaps.
- When a sound is built from concatenated segments or alternating states, crossfade every boundary. Before submitting, inspect the waveform at every seed and toggle for vertical notches or dropouts. Also make sure no parameter extreme lets a masking layer bury the sound's defining element.
- When you model an impact on a surface, check the waveform tail for evenly spaced oscillation. A long, regular ringing decay means a resonator or filter is dominating, and the sound will read as a struck instrument instead of the material contact you were asked for. Damp the resonance heavily and let shaped broadband noise carry the tail.
