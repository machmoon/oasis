- When content grows toward a knob's maximum, reflow or paginate rather than silently hiding optional sections. Render the max case and check that every block from the default render is still present before shipping.
- When you de-emphasise secondary elements (inactive screens, background cards), use scale, position, blur or a uniform overlay rather than fading text colour. Keep any text that remains visible at legible contrast in every brand theme. Also check that each preset differs visibly from the defaults before shipping it.
- When a component takes arbitrary brand colours into a gradient, interpolate in a perceptual space (e.g. OKLCH) or insert a controlled midpoint stop. Also derive accent and foreground colours from contrast against the actual local background, so complementary brand pairs don't collapse into a muddy grey middle with clashing accents.
- Derive container and highlight surface tones from the page background with an enforced minimum contrast step, rather than from fixed tints or alpha overlays, so cards stay distinct and highlights stay clean on any brand palette.
- When a toggle expresses a state (paid, overdue, draft), surface it once at the highest-hierarchy position and let other components stay neutral. Repeating the same signal across several elements reads as noise, not emphasis.
- Any decorative micro-text, like text inside stamps, seals or badges, must render at a legible minimum size at defaults and at max-density scales. Otherwise drop it. Unreadable filler reads as noise and cheapens an otherwise polished asset.
- Placeholder data in a template must stay internally consistent under every knob setting. If you show quantities, prices and totals, derive them from one source (line total = unit price × quantity; subtotal = sum of line totals; header count = sum of quantities), so the numbers never contradict each other at defaults or at any preset.
- Never derive focus, active or selection styling from the brand colour alone. Keep error and success colours as fixed semantic tokens, and test a red or orange brand to confirm that 'focused' and 'invalid' still look different at a glance.
- When deriving surface tokens from a brand background, enforce a minimum luminance step and a fixed direction. In light themes, cards and inputs must be lighter than the canvas. This keeps elevation and text contrast intact on tinted backgrounds.
- When mapping brand colours onto an illustration, bind each colour to a semantic role with guarded hue and contrast ranges (e.g. water stays blue-cyan, paths keep contrast against both ground and background) rather than pouring raw brand accents into whatever slot is free. Then verify that every knob listed in the brief produces a visible change at its extremes.
- In isometric scenes, reserve a clearance zone in front of every focal object and keep vertical props such as poles and posts out of it. Occluders in projection always cut across the feature the viewer most needs to read.
- When a decorative palette is derived from the brand colour, build it from tints and shades of the brand hue, anchored to the current mode's surface colour. Never use complementary or rotated hues. Always test with a high-chroma brand on dark mode, so the hero art can't outshine the UI or produce clashing hue pairs.
- Procedural scatter (trees, props, figures, signage) must be placed against explicit exclusion zones derived from the functional geometry (tracks, paths, walkways, labels). Those zones must be recomputed whenever a knob changes the layout's length or count, so nothing ever sits on, through or tangled with a primary element.
- Make every exposed knob's extreme visibly different from its default, and verify this in the max-range render. A knob whose range produces no perceptible change is clutter that erodes buyer trust in all the other controls.
- When deriving a fill from brand colours, build a tonal ramp within one hue family (lighter and darker shades of the brand colour) instead of a multi-stop gradient between unrelated hues. Cross-hue gradients turn muddy or neon with arbitrary brand inputs and destroy the premium finish the asset depends on.
- Make every state a knob can select (active, hover, selected) come from one style rule applied by index, and render the first, middle and last options. A state that only looks right at its default position is a bug buyers will find immediately.
- Derive every annotation's anchor (tooltip pointer, marker dot, highlight band) from the same computed geometry as the shape it annotates, and re-render at every knob extreme to confirm the annotation still touches its target.
- Bind brand colour roles only to elements that are naturally arbitrary in colour (sails, awnings, doors, stripes). Keep elements with a physical colour identity, like water, sky and foliage, inside a constrained hue range so a brand palette can tint them but never repaint them into something unrecognisable.
- Presets must visibly exercise every headline knob named in the brief (e.g. season, time of day), not just recolour one surface. If a knob is in the brief and no preset or probe shows it doing something distinct, reviewers will treat it as missing.
- When a knob shrinks the space available to embedded text, such as a donut hole or a card body, define an explicit fallback. For example, drop the secondary caption below a size threshold rather than letting it wrap and shrink, so extreme settings still look deliberate.
- Selected-row state must remain distinguishable from zebra striping by more than a small luminance step, especially in monochrome themes. Pair the selected tint with a second cue, such as a leading accent bar or a stronger tint, and verify that both alternating and selected rows survive when the selection count covers every visible row.
- Any scattered or density-driven element must be placed against an exclusion map of every other footprint (paths, props, focal objects, each other), and must be checked at maximum density, where collisions first become visible.
- Treat callouts and tooltips as layout that must not hide data. Position them by measuring every mark in the plot, not just the anchored one. Prefer empty headroom above the series, flip sides or shift vertically when they would cover any bar or point, and check each preset and extreme specifically for data hidden under overlays.
- Paint decorative backgrounds (glows, gradients, blobs) on the full-bleed root layer, never inside a single column, so they don't hard-clip at layout seams. Then verify that every declared knob produces a visibly different render at its extremes.
- Position floating overlays such as tooltips and callouts against the actual content at the hover point, not against a fixed offset. Compute the bounding box of every active marker and every nearby line segment, then flip or shift the overlay until it touches none of them. Verify this at maximum series count, where the free space is smallest.
- Never reuse the brand accent as a text-bearing fill without checking contrast: for every surface that holds text or icons (avatars, badges, illustration details), compute the foreground from the actual fill colour against a minimum contrast ratio. Do not assume one fixed light/dark choice works for all brands.
- When a count or size knob grows a central structure, regenerate the placement of decorative scatter (trees, props, signage) against the new footprint. Add collision checks so props never intersect the structure, and scale key labels with the structure.
- In isometric scenes, keep tall background props out of the silhouette of foreground objects, or offset them clearly so they cannot be misread as stacked on top. Overlapping depth layers that align vertically read as one object.
- Every knob in the brief should produce a visibly different render at its extreme. If alignment or blur strength looks identical at max, the knob is either broken or too subtle to sell, so widen its range or make its effect legible in the preview.
- In data visualisations, either derive the geometry from the data or make the decorative shape explicitly a knob. Lay out annotations by anchoring each one to the exact element it describes. Then verify that adding items reflows within fixed margins instead of extending past the canvas.
- Never hard-code foreground colour per element when the background is derived from a theme or brand colour. Compute text colour from the actual fill's contrast (target ≥4.5:1 for small text) so every generated tint stays legible in light and dark brands. Also keep semantic colours like priority or status in hue families that tag palettes are barred from reusing.
- When auto-picking text colour on an accent fill, choose it by computed contrast ratio against both candidates (white vs. dark) rather than a fixed luminance cutoff, and test mid-luminance pastels like periwinkle and sky blue, where fixed cutoffs fail most often.
- When a knob adds items to a shared row (legend entries, tabs, chips), design the overflow state deliberately: balanced wrapping, a column grid, or relocation below the plot. Never let items spill one-by-one onto an orphaned line.
- When a value is formatted at defaults, check it at both ends of its range. Signs, padding and labels like '−0:00' must stay consistent, and indicators like playheads need breathing room at 0% and 100%.
- Before shipping, render every knob at its minimum and maximum and diff them against the defaults. If an extreme is not visibly and meaningfully different, widen the range or cut the knob. Derive every secondary accent from the brand colour so brand swaps never leave orphaned hues.
- When a component reflows into a compact layout under load, keep its visual primitives (corner radius, button style) identical to the default. Reserve clearance for persistent indicator columns so inline controls never crowd them.
- When a sequential colour ramp encodes rank or size, anchor it so the step furthest from the base colour moves away from the surface colour, not toward it. Also check that the smallest or last element still meets a minimum contrast against the background in both light and dark themes.
- Derive shadows, outlines and other low-contrast elements from the actual background luminance, and render-check them on a dark theme. A fixed grey shadow or dark outline that works on light backgrounds will show up as a lighter stain or disappear entirely on near-black.
- Make your presets exercise the enum knobs that define the asset's value (scenario, severity, shape variants), not just colour themes. A knob that no preview ever shows changing is indistinguishable from a knob that doesn't exist.
- When a knob regenerates base geometry such as a street grid or terrain, derive every dependent overlay (routes, pins, labels) from that same generated geometry so they stay snapped to it. Otherwise the asset only looks correct at the default seed.
- Never hardcode stroke or ink colour in illustrations. Derive outline and facial-feature colour from contrast against both the fill it sits on and the canvas, and check that hair, skin and line work stay separable from the background in a dark theme.
- When a scenario depends on an object being tipped, rotated or partly hidden, check at thumbnail size that its key identifying features (rim, opening, handle orientation) still read unmistakably as that object. Re-centre each variant's visual mass within its backdrop rather than reusing one layout for all variants.
- Treat a range knob's extreme as a design state you must art-direct. If max differs from default only by a few added decorations, either widen the range so it produces a distinctly different composition or cut the knob.
- Every named preset must produce a visibly distinct render from the defaults. If a preset is the default, don't ship it as a separate option. Spend that slot on a meaningfully different configuration.
