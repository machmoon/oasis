- When a brand or theme colour drives recolouring, map it only to accent and trim parts. Keep semantically fixed materials (water, foliage, skin, glass) within a believable hue range. Also clamp surface lightness so dark themes still show face-to-face shading on 3D forms.
- When mapping brand colours onto an illustrative asset, bind them only to designated accent surfaces (trim, balconies, awnings). Keep semantic materials like foliage, water and glass on fixed or tightly constrained hues. Also preserve the value hierarchy between roof, walls and accents, so any brand palette still reads as the same object.
- Map brand colours to surfaces by role with luminance guards. Large surfaces like roofs and walls should get desaturated, clamped tints of the brand colour rather than the raw value. Every major mass should keep visible contrast against both the canvas and its neighbouring faces. Check this in both the light and dark brand probes before submitting.
- When brand colours drive surfaces with directional face shading, compute each face's tone relative to the input's luminance and clamp it to a usable range. Near-black or near-white brand inputs must still yield clearly separated top, left and right faces, otherwise 3D forms collapse into flat silhouettes.
- Separate brand-driven colour roles from semantically fixed objects (food, skin, materials like brick). For every brand-mapped role, clamp lightness so faces keep their isometric value steps and stay distinct from neighbours. Then verify the hero element still reads as itself under both a very light and a very dark brand.
- Give a colour knob a `role` only if it is paint: walls, awnings, trim, signage, body panels. Never give a role to a material (water, foliage, glass, stone, metal, wood, lamps); leave those role-less so Brand Mode cannot turn water red or leaves purple.
- Keep value structure under any brand: dark roles go on small accents, not on the biggest surfaces, so a dark brand never turns a whole building black with a white roof.
- Every range knob must change the silhouette visibly at min and max, and every toggle must change something you can see in the default thumbnail. A floors or width knob must change the actual mass, not just detail.
- Fill the footprint the brief gives and keep the main mass dominant; do not add tall thin poles or spires that shrink the model in its thumbnail.
- At maximum counts, items must still have room: space them by their real size and cap the count to what fits the footprint.
- Every distinct material (water tanks, rooftop units, planters) uses the kit palette, never an unrelated saturated colour.
